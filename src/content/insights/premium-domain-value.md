Premium domain pricing is driven less by hype and more by scarcity, clarity, and commercial intent. In 2026, buyers still pay up for names that are short, category-exact, and easy to say on a jobsite phone call.

## What moves price

1. **Exact match** — the domain contains the primary keyword prospects already search.
2. **Length** — fewer characters mean cleaner logos, SMS links, and paid-search display URLs.
3. **Category TLD** — extensions like `.construction` can outperform generic alternatives when the audience is trade-specific.
4. **Local anchor** — a city code or market name adds defensibility for service-area businesses.

## Why ABQ.Construction sits in the premium band

The name pairs Albuquerque’s widely recognized city code with the category every builder already sells against. Comparable city + trade combinations rarely appear on the open market, and once acquired they tend to stay parked inside operating brands rather than recycle through aftermarket listings.

Asking prices for that class of asset reflect replacement difficulty, not vanity. If your growth plan depends on owning the phrase clients already use, valuation should start from that scarcity—not from generic `.com` comps alone.
